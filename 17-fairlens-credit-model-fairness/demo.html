Portfolio prototype on synthetic data, not a production case study. The groups are labels the generator made up and encode no real protected class. The scorecard is a stub, not a trained model, and no model is used anywhere. AIR is a screening statistic, not a legal finding; 0.80 is a convention from employment guidance, not a credit-law standard. Every number on this page is generated from the scripts in src/.

Utility delivered

Utility = (fairness tests run with FairLens − with accuracy only) ÷ tests × credit decisions a year

🟢 Measured on this synthetic set: the accuracy-only view runs 0 of 6 fairness tests (by definition). The pack runs 6 of 6 and 5 of 6 return REVIEW or FAIL at the policy cutoff 190. The lowest AIR is 0.750 (Group C), and approval rates sit 20.9 points apart behind a model-card accuracy of 87.1%. Neutralizing the postcode points lifts the lowest AIR from 0.750 to 0.871 and accuracy from 87.1% to 89.2%.

🟡 Modeled: 20,000 credit decisions a year under this scorecard (an assumption, not a statistic) gives 1.00 × 20,000 = 20,000 decisions a year under a model with a full fairness test pack. 495 of 6,000 test-set decisions (8.25%) turn on the postcode points; at the same share the modeled volume would be about 1,650. The test set is dense on purpose, so that is not a frequency estimate.

🔴 What it still gets wrong: AIR is a screening statistic, and the search for less discriminatory alternatives is one ablation. Real credit data has no group labels: with 14.8% of labels wrong at random the screen passes (lowest AIR 0.817) when it fails on the true labels (0.750). The reason-code pair check passes (+3.3 points against its control) while 295 declines are caused by the postcode points; with 4 reasons listed, 18 of them omit the postcode reason.
