# Data for project 72

Synthetic. Generated by `_generate.py` with a fixed seed (72), so a rerun reproduces every file byte for byte. Nothing here comes from a real bank, customer, payment rail or employer, and no rate in these files is a real rate.

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `payments.csv` | 12,000 real-time payments, the features a bank could see at the moment of payment: `payment_id`, `amount` (dollars), `usual_amount` (the customer's typical payment), `payee_age_days` (0 = first payment to this payee), `hour` (0 to 23), `call_active` (1 = the customer is on a phone call), `device_new` (1 = a device the bank has not seen) |
| `truth.csv` | What was planted, one row per payment: `is_scam` (0 or 1), `scenario`, and two stored draws, `u_abandon` and `u_evade`, each between 0 and 1. The draws decide a held customer's response, so two policies see the same customers. The rules in `src/` read `is_scam`, `scenario` and the draws only to score an outcome, never to decide a tier |
| `assumptions.json` | The invented behaviour of customers under a hold. Every number in it is an assumption |

The generator draws the scenario first and then the features from a distribution that belongs to that scenario. The risk rules in `src/scam_friction_sim.py` are separate code that never sees the scenario.

Planted scenarios (12,000 payments):

| Scenario | Scam? | Count | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ordinary` | no | 10,820 | Mostly established payees, amounts near the customer's usual |
| `big_first_time` | no | 330 | Hard negative: a legitimate first payment to a new payee at 3 to 14 times the usual amount (a deposit, a purchase); 20% are made while on a call |
| `night_new_device` | no | 170 | Hard negative: a legitimate payment from a new device in the small hours |
| `on_call` | no | 350 | Hard negative: a legitimate payment made during a phone call |
| `coached_live` | yes | 120 | A caller coaches the victim: large, first payment to a new payee, usually on a call; 10% are made small to look ordinary |
| `romance_invest` | yes | 90 | A slow-burn scam: moderate amounts, new or young payee, evenings, rarely on a call |
| `takeover` | yes | 60 | The account holder is not the one paying: new device, night hours, large amounts. Counted as a scam here because the owner can cancel during a hold |
| `known_payee` | yes | 60 | Hard case the rules should miss: a payee the customer has paid for 20 to 400 days, ordinary amounts |

330 of 12,000 payments are scams. That share is set far above anything real so the counts are big enough to read, and the ratio of legitimate holds to scams stopped depends on it directly; step 3 shows the same rule at one-tenth and one-hundredth of that share.

## Assumptions (🟡 modeled: none of these is measured)

| Name | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `hold_length` (`hold_minutes`) | 20 minutes | The scenario named in the project idea. It multiplies waiting time only; the model gives the stop probability no dependence on length |
| `warn_minutes` | 0.5 | Time a legitimate customer spends on an in-app warning |
| `legit_abandon_on_hold` | 0.03 | Chance a held legitimate payment is abandoned |
| `legit_abandon_on_warn` | 0.005 | Same, after a warning only |
| `warn_factor` | 0.3 | A warning has this share of the effect of a hold on a scam payment |
| `coach_rate` | 0.2 | Chance that a scammer in touch with the victim has coached them to wait out the hold, which cancels the stop |
| `p_abandon`, `coached_live` | 0.4 | Chance a held victim gives up |
| `p_abandon`, `romance_invest` | 0.1 | The victim believes the relationship and waits |
| `p_abandon`, `takeover` | 0.35 | The real owner sees the hold and cancels |
| `p_abandon`, `known_payee` | 0.15 | Used only if such a payment is ever held |
| `human_scammer` | true for three, false for `takeover` | Whether coaching can apply |

Risk score weights (the author's, not fitted): first payment to a new payee 3, payee 1 to 7 days old 1, amount at least 3 times usual 2, at least 1.5 times 1, amount of 1,000 or more 1, call in progress 2, new device 2, hour 0 to 5 1. The blanket rule holds a first payment to a new payee of 500 or more.

## Scripts that read this data

`step_01_no_hold.py`, `step_02_blanket_hold.py`, `step_03_deficiencies_exposed.py` and `step_04_scored_hold.py` read all three files. `scam_friction_sim.py --demo` reads them too.
