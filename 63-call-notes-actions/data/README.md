# Data for project 63

Synthetic. Generated by `_generate.py` with a fixed seed, so a rerun reproduces every file byte for byte. Nothing here comes from a real bank, client, employer or CRM. Client surnames are invented.

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `notes.csv` | 120 call notes, 705 sentences, one row per sentence: `note_id`, `client_id`, `call_date`, `sent_id`, `text` |
| `sentence_roles.csv` | The role the generator gave each sentence: `commit`, `commit_compound`, `conditional`, `declined`, `declined_phrasal`, `hedged`, `hedged_soft`, `negated`, `negated_phrasal`, `past`, `info` |
| `truth_actions.csv` | The 258 actions the notes really commit to: `action_id`, `note_id`, `action_type`, `owner`, `due`, `subject`, `amount`, `sent_id` |
| `candidates.csv` | The 440 actions the stub extractor proposed, each with a cited sentence: `cand_id`, `note_id`, `action_type`, `owner`, `due`, `subject`, `amount`, `cite_sent_id`, `cite_quote` |
| `candidate_truth.csv` | The planted cause per candidate: `defect`, `valid` (1 when the candidate matches a planted action), `truth_action_id`. The label comes from the generator, not from the checker |
| `example_notes.csv`, `example_candidates.csv` | The worked example behind `python3 call_notes_actions.py --demo`: one note, seven candidates |

## Action types

`send_document`, `await_document` (the client owes the document), `callback`, `schedule_review`, `limit_request` (carries an amount), `log_complaint`, `update_preference`. The first five are commitments by a person and have an owner (`rm` or `client`) and usually a due date.

## How the candidates are produced

Each note is built from sentence templates, so the role of every sentence is known when it is written. The stub extractor is a seeded corruption process over that truth. For a planted action it emits the correct candidate or, at fixed rates, one with a defect. For a sentence that must not become an action it emits a candidate that cites it anyway.

Planted causes (440 candidates): valid 149, valid_reformatted_quote 26 (the quote is tidied: curly apostrophe, doubled space, cut short with an ellipsis, lower case), valid_negation_elsewhere 4 (a valid action in a sentence that also negates something else), valid_spelled_amount 5 (the amount is written in words), fabricated_cite 14, wrong_sentence 38, slot_changed 18, owner_flipped 4, conditional 31, negated 19, past 38, declined 21, hedged 13, clause_swap 13, and three kinds the rules do not read: negated_phrasal 8 ("hold off"), declined_phrasal 22 ("turned down"), hedged_soft 17 ("I will try to"). The three valid kinds after `valid` are the hard negatives: each could raise a false alarm. `clause_swap`, `negated_phrasal` and `hedged_soft` are the ones that can slip through; `declined_phrasal` is stopped by the owner rule, not by a word list.

## Assumptions (🟡 modeled)

- The stub's rates are mine: 6% of planted actions are never proposed; for the rest, 60% correct, 12% tidied quote, 7% invented quote, 5% wrong sentence, 10% changed date or amount, 6% flipped owner (a rate with nothing to change falls back to correct); 70% of sentences that must not become an action get a candidate; 30% of the actions in a two-clause sentence take a slot from the other clause. A real extractor will differ.
- A weekday name means its first occurrence after the call date; "tomorrow" is the day after; "D Month" takes the call's year, or the next year when that date would fall before the call.
- Calls fall on weekdays between 2026-10-01 and 2026-10-26. Due dates are 1 to 12 days after the call and never on a weekend.
- Dollar amounts are whole thousands from 10,000 to 100,000.
- No regulation or standard supplies a parameter. The word lists in `src/call_notes_actions.py` are my own.
