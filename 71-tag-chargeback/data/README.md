# Data for project 71

Synthetic. Generated by `_generate.py` with a fixed seed, so a rerun reproduces every file byte for byte. Nothing here comes from a real bill, bank, cloud account or employer. The cost-center names and the account names are invented.

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `billing_export.csv` | 1284 billing lines for one month: `line_id`, `usage_date`, `account_id`, `service`, `resource_id`, `usage_type`, `cost_usd`, `tags_json` |
| `billing_truth.csv` | The planted owner per line: `true_cost_center` (a cost center, `SHARED` for a shared service, `NONE` for a zero-cost line), `defect_kind`, and `compliant` (yes, no or exempt, read straight off the planted tags). It comes from the story the generator tells, not from the allocation rules |
| `cost_centers.csv` | 14 cost centers, two of them closed (`active` is false) |
| `accounts.csv` | 12 accounts: 8 dedicated to one cost center each, 4 shared with no default |
| `policy.json` | Required tags `cost-center` (checked against the registry), `app` and `env`; four shared services; an unallocated limit |
| `example_bill.csv` | The 14-line worked example behind `python3 tag_chargeback.py --demo`, one line per rule or refusal reason |

Assumptions, all 🟡 modeled and mine, none taken from a rule or standard: the three required tags and the alias spellings in `policy.json`; the unallocated limit of 10.0%; splitting shared services in proportion to what the other rules allocated; the weekly line grain and the spend tiers in the generator.

Story behind the data: 43 apps, each owned by one cost center, 258 resources (6 per app). Three apps belong to one team and sit in another team's dedicated account. Then defects are planted per resource, and the truth is recorded before they are applied.

Planted defects, in lines: clean 608; alias_key 48 (a listed alias such as `CostCenter`); value_format 56 (lower case or spaces); alias_unlisted 24 (a key the policy does not list); value_nohyphen 16 (`CC1004`); unknown_value 20 (an id not in the registry); closed_cc 24 (a stale tag on a closed cost center); conflict 20 (two tag keys disagree); wrong_valid 24 (a valid tag for the wrong team); missing 64 (no cost-center key); empty_value 16 (key present, value empty); partial_tag 40 (tagged late in the month); wrong_default 36 (untagged resource of one team in another team's dedicated account); foreign_tagged 36 (the same placement, tagged correctly); child_untagged 202 (data transfer and snapshot lines with no tags); credit 10; shared_service 16; zero_cost 24. Separately, about 16% of resources lose or misspell the `env` or `app` tag, which lowers compliance and does not affect allocation.

Hard on purpose, because the rules cannot see them: wrong_valid, wrong_default, alias_unlisted and value_nohyphen. Hard negatives, which a careless check would flag: zero_cost, shared_service, credit, foreign_tagged, value_format and alias_key.
