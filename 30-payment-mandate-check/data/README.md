# Data for project 30

Synthetic. Generated by `_generate.py` with a fixed seed, so a rerun reproduces every file byte for byte. Nothing here comes from a real customer, bank, payee or employer; every company name is made up from syllables.

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `mandates.json` | 14 mandates: customer, the one agent they were issued to, currency, per-payment limit, daily limit, confirmation threshold (null on 3 of them), validity dates, revocation time, rails, categories, and 4 to 6 payees each with the account the mandate pins |
| `instructions.csv` | 475 payment instructions, sorted by `received_at` and numbered `S-00001` onward in that order. `submission_id` is the gateway's number; `instruction_id` is the agent's own key and repeats across mandates on purpose |
| `truth.csv` | The planted scenario per instruction: `kind`, `truth_decision` (RELEASE, HOLD or REJECT) and `group_id`. The label comes from the scenario that was planted, not from the checker. The generator does not import it |
| `example_mandates.json`, `example_instructions.csv` | The worked example behind `python3 payment_mandate_check.py --demo`: one mandate, seven instructions |

Planted truth: 249 RELEASE, 62 HOLD, 164 REJECT.

Scenarios (instructions): RELEASE: routine 157, cap_fill 35, at_confirm_threshold 10, recurring_gap 10, at_per_payment_limit 8, hostile_memo 6, at_daily_cap 5, last_second 4, before_revocation 4, first_second 2, and the first piece of each of 8 split payments. HOLD: over_confirm_threshold 20, split_below_confirm 16 (the second and third piece of each of 8 groups), near_duplicate 14, payee_account_changed 12. REJECT: over_limit 20, expired 14, payee_not_listed 14, revoked 12, payee_lookalike 12, daily_cap_breach 10, duplicate_id 10, wrong_agent 10, unknown_mandate 8, payee_other_mandate 8, wrong_currency 8, wrong_rail 8, wrong_category 8, bad_amount 8, memo_contradicts_category 8, not_yet_valid 6.

Two kinds of scenario are hard on purpose.
- Hard negatives, which a good check releases: an amount exactly at the confirmation threshold or the per-payment limit, a payment that reaches the daily limit to the cent (`at_daily_cap`), a payment at the very second a mandate expires or starts, a payment seconds before a revocation, the same payee and amount again after a long gap, memo and payee-name text that tries to look like output or a command, and instruction ids that repeat across mandates.
- Hard misses, which the rules release although the truth says otherwise: `split_below_confirm` (three pieces to one payee within minutes, each under the confirmation threshold, the total over it) and `memo_contradicts_category` (a permitted declared category, a memo that says gift cards for resale).

Assumptions in the generator, all mine and none from a rule or a source (the README tags them yellow): a near-duplicate means the same payee, amount and currency within 600 seconds of a released payment; the daily limit runs over a UTC calendar day of the gateway's `received_at`; a held or rejected payment does not use up the day; every episode that releases money gets a day of its own, so no daily total mixes two scenarios. The same author wrote the data and the rules, so a score on this set is a ceiling for these rules, not a rate on real traffic.
