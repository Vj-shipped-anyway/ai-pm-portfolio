# Data for project 70

Synthetic. Generated by `_generate.py` with a fixed seed (`SEED = 7020261024`), so a rerun reproduces every file byte for byte. Nothing here comes from a real lender, applicant, postcode, bank or model. The group labels are synthetic and encode no real protected class. The scorecard is a hand-written points scorecard, a stub, not a trained model.

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `case_scores.csv` | 29,460 scored applicants in 36 portfolios. Columns: `case_id`, `applicant_id`, `group`, `score`, `bad_outcome`, `postcode_points`, `region_points`, `reason_1`, `reason_2`, `reason_3` |
| `case_truth.csv` | The planted mechanism per portfolio: `mechanism`, `proxy_planted`, `proxy_predictive`, `legit_shift_scale`, `postcode_max_points`, `region_max_points`, `smallest_group_size`, `applicants`, `cutoff`, `redraws`. The label comes from the generator's parameters, not from any test |
| `example_scores.csv`, `example_params.json` | Portfolio C19 without the `case_id` column, and the cutoff and proxy settings behind `python3 fairlens_pack.py --demo` |

## How a portfolio is built

Each applicant gets four legitimate quality draws (income, debt ratio, history, utilization), turned into points by quarter of the component maximum (70, 65, 60 and 60 points), plus postcode points and, in some portfolios, region points. The score is the sum. The decline reasons are the three components with the most points lost against their maximum, in that order. The policy cutoff is the score at the 30th percentile of the portfolio, so about 70% are approved.

`bad_outcome` is drawn from a logistic function of the four legitimate quality draws only. The postcode band enters it in one mechanism (`proxy_predictive`) and in no other. The group never enters it directly. The tests in `src/fairlens_pack.py` never see these parameters.

## Planted mechanisms (36 portfolios)

| Mechanism | Portfolios | What was planted | Planted proxy |
|---|---|---|---|
| `clean` | 5 | Groups share every distribution; the postcode points are unrelated to group | no |
| `legit_gap` | 6 | Groups differ in the legitimate inputs, so real default risk differs; no proxy | no |
| `small_group_noise` | 6 | Groups share every distribution; the smallest group has 30 applicants. Each portfolio is redrawn until that portfolio's lowest approval ratio is under 0.80 (6, 4, 10, 2, 10 and 1 redraws; see `redraws`). Hard negative: there is no gap to find | no |
| `proxy_gap` | 8 | The postcode points track group (40 to 60 maximum points) and have no effect on default | yes |
| `proxy_predictive` | 5 | The postcode points track group and also predict default (hard negative: a correlated feature that earns its place) | no |
| `two_proxies` | 3 | Postcode and region points both track group, 28 maximum points each; the pack is pointed at the postcode column only (hard case: a known limit) | yes |
| `quiet_proxy` | 3 | A postcode proxy worth 8, 10 or 12 maximum points (hard case: too small to move the ratio) | yes |

Group sizes are 450, 280 and 100 applicants (30 in the smallest group of `small_group_noise`).

## Seed and parameter choice (stated plainly)

- `SEED` was set once and the generator run on it. I did not search over seeds.
- I did adjust parameters after looking at the pack's output on earlier draws: the group sizes (they were 250, 150 and 50 at first, and the intervals were too wide to separate anything), the proxy weights, the predictive coefficients and the shift scales of `legit_gap`. So the data is shaped to show the effects. It is a test set, not an estimate of how often any of them happens in real portfolios.
- The demo portfolio is C19, the first `proxy_gap` portfolio with a 45-point postcode weight. I picked 45 points because the first `proxy_gap` portfolio (C18) is a weak case the pack misses, and I wanted the demo to show the mechanism. It is not a typical row: T4 marks 7 of the 8 `proxy_gap` portfolios FAIL.

## Assumptions (🟡 modeled, not read from any rule)

Every threshold in `src/fairlens_pack.py`: the 0.80 ratio screen, the 10-point approval gap, the 30-point cutoff sweep and its 10-point headroom, the 0.05 lift and 1-point accuracy cost in the ablation, the 5-point score band and the 15 and 30-point margins in the reason-pair check, the 10% and 50% naming shares, the 15 and 10 minimum counts, 400 resamples, and three reasons listed per decline. Change them and the verdicts change.

## What is not claimed

- No claim that these groups, postcodes or default rates resemble any real population.
- No claim that a real scorecard's gap would be this size, or that a real proxy would sit in a column the tool can be pointed at.
- No claim that a ratio under 0.80 is a legal finding. It is a screening statistic.
